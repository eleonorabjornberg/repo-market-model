## 1. Executive summary: top three

**1. The honest baseline is not B1, and no paired test of the registration candidate against a transparent model exists (HIGH).** A zero-parameter forecast (persistence anchored on the last *ordinary* day, h-day changes pooled by the target's day type) beats B1 by 15.6% of grid CRPS on development days, 7.0% post-2020, and 1.1% on ordinary anchors — verified on proxy; the repo's `b1_recentred` row gives 14.8% (`docs/pivot/spread-mr-variants-result.md`). The one-parameter mean-reversion model (#686) beats B1 by 22.3%. The 3% MME against B1 is cleared five times over by a benchmark that knows nothing. #741 is filed as "candidate evidence (a) for a §13 change" and awaits a decision; what is *not* filed is the test that decides whether `gbm_funding` carries anything beyond the transparent models: a paired grid-CRPS test of `gbm_funding` against `spread_mr_ctx` and against B1′, pooled and by target type. Cost 1–2 days. Expected value: high, because it decides what the registration claims.

**2. The effective sample post-2020 is about five independent 250-day windows, and the critical-value calibration destroys the dependence it is meant to model (HIGH).** The score-difference series the test resamples has, post-2020 (where every confirmation day falls), autocorrelation 0.73 at lag 1, 0.31 at lag 21, 0.16 at lag 42 (verified on proxy). Its Politis–White block length is about 108 days, not 10. Newey–West at lag 10 understates the standard error by about 2×. The calibration windows are built from circular 10-day blocks (`scoring_protocol.py:702-707`) or the stationary bootstrap at mean block 10 (#886), both capping dependence at a few weeks; on *contiguous* 250-day post-2020 windows the 95th percentile of the studentized statistic is 6.05 against 2.37 (circular) and 2.93 (stationary), and 41–43% of contiguous windows cross the calibrated values under no true skill. #949 (filed today) measures blocks 5/10/20; the new evidence is that all three are an order of magnitude short, that calibration must use contiguous windows, and that there are too few to calibrate a 250-day test at all. What a quant does instead is §4.2.

**3. The primary score is a few-days statistic that over-weights the extreme quantiles of an integer target; a scale-free, as-of-standardised CRPS is the one lever that gave the test any power (HIGH).** One decision day (2019-09-18) is 6.3% of B1's whole development-history score; the top 1% of days are 24% (verified on proxy; the repo records B1 = 297.6 bp that day, `docs/research/direct-twin-449-result.md:21`). Yearly variance of the spread runs from 2.3 bp² (2023) to 458 bp² (2019), so pooled mean(d)/mean(S_B1) is a variance-weighted average of regimes. The 23-level grid gives the outer six levels 26% of the weight against 15% under the CRPS integral (2.5× at 1%/99%; `scoring_protocol.py:62-66`, `metrics.py:795-797`, acknowledged at `metrics.py:805-817`), on a whole-bp target with 30% of days on two values. #733's standardized designs D4/D5 raised 250-day power at 3% from 0% to 11–13% and were rejected on size — but that size failure came from the calibration generator (item 2), not from standardizing. Not tracked (grep "standardi(s|z)ed/scaled/relative CRPS" in the bodies: nothing). Cost ~2 days.

## 3. Lens findings, in the brief's order

### 3.1 The target

**Is SOFR − IORB right given IORB steps and the floor/ceiling?** For the confirmation era, yes. Across the 28 IORB step days in the panel the spread's one-day change is 0 on 9 days and within ±1 bp on 19 of 28; the 75-bp hikes of 2022 leave it untouched (verified, work-C). The steps that move it are the ones a practitioner expects: the 2019 IOER technical adjustments (−5 bp inside the range) and 2024-12-19, when the ON RRP rate was cut 30 bp against IORB's 25, so the spread stepped −8 → −10 and stayed. Those are the IORB − ON RRP width changes #948 lists. A corridor-position target matters in the floor-anchored years 2021–24 (modal value −10 bp on 38–52% of each year's days; the top three values hold 78–92% of 2021–23, verified); for the 2026+ confirmation days, with ON RRP take-up near zero, SOFR − IORB is the natural coordinate. **Tracked: #948.** Verified independently: `RRPONTSYAWARD` is declared in `metadata/sources.json:112,188` and listed at `ingest.py:74`, but no fixture carries it and no panel code reads it (grep over `tests/fixtures/`, `data.py`, `contract.py`, `baseline.py`: nothing), so SOFR − ON RRP is not computable from held data today; the announcement-dated register is the right source.

**The ceiling.** The SRF minimum bid is the top of the target range, IORB + 10 bp (bodies line 1073, by construction via `DFEDTARU`). The panel says it leaks: post-2020-04 there are 24 days at or above +10 bp and 5 at or above +20 (max +32 on 2025-10-31), all in 2024–25 (verified). A "stochastic distance to the ceiling" is therefore a model of *leakage over* the SRF rate, not a reflecting barrier (avenue D).

**Discreteness.** The spread is an integer in bp on all 2104 rows (verified: `(np.abs(s-np.round(s))>1e-6).sum()` = 0). Mass: −10 bp on 372 days (17.7%), −9 on 268 (12.7%), 0 on 148, −1 on 144. Consequences: (i) the pinball loss has a flat minimum between consecutive mass points, so interpolated empirical quantiles (`protocol_benchmarks.py:67-86`) are not penalised; (ii) at the 1%/99% levels, where the grid gives 2.5× the integral's weight, the forecast quantile sits on one of a handful of integers and the loss is a step function of which — the noisiest part of the score gets the most weight; (iii) in 2021–23 B1's mean CRPS is 0.711 bp (`h5-step3-diagnoses-result.md`), so ratios there are ratios of rounding; (iv) a deterministic PIT is non-uniform for a perfect forecast when the outcome ties many quantiles (`scripts/h5_step3_707.py:168-176` takes the bracket midpoint) — the 2021–23 "0.33–0.42 of PIT at or below 0.10" is probably real (ties push PIT to the middle, not the lower tail) but should be re-read with a randomised PIT (avenue F; #247 randomised ties for coverage, not PIT — suspected gap).

### 3.2 Persistence and the honest baseline

**B1 is weak for a known reason** (#741, tracked): it anchors on the last public spread, which the day after a month-end or tax date is the spike, and adds h-day changes that include every spike in the trailing two years. On the proxy: B1 2.315 bp; recentred on the last ordinary day 1.969 (+15.0%; repo +14.8%); recentred with changes pooled by the target's day type (30-value minimum, else all) 1.954 (+15.6%). By anchor type: +35% after month-ends, +66% after quarter-ends, +75% after tax dates, **+1.1% on ordinary anchors** (verified on proxy, `b1prime.py`). Post-2020 +7.0%; 2024–26 +7.8%. **This zero-parameter forecast is what a quant calls persistence.** AR with calendar dummies is the next rung and the repo has it: `spread_mr_level10` (`level + phi^k (last − level) + eps_h`, one grid-searched phi, `spread_mean_reversion.py:1-21`) at +22.3% over B1; `spread_mr_ctx` at +23.5%.

**The missing paired comparison.** `gbm_funding` has +25.7% over B1 on ordinary targets (`h5-step4-result.md`) and a pooled mean grid CRPS of 1.826 bp on 1868 days (`direct-twin-449-result.md`), while `spread_mr_level10` has 1.778 on 1855 days. Different day sets, not paired (`spread-mean-reversion/report.md:213-215` says so), so nobody can say whether the one-parameter model is level with the gbm. On ordinary anchors B1′ ≈ B1, so the gbm's ordinary-*target* gain may survive; pooled, its skill against the honest benchmark is ≈ 25% − 15% ≈ 10% at most (suspected, unpaired).

**Futures-implied expectation:** licensing rules it out; over five days it is the calendar plus the level, which is what B1′ encodes.

**Are B1 and B2 the right bars?** No. B2's quarter-end quantiles are the 23 interpolated quantiles of **four** values (4 quarter-ends per trailing year, verified; `b2_forecast`, `protocol_benchmarks.py:176-209`); its 1%/99% levels are the sample min and max. #762 measured the consequence (quarter-end 90% coverage 0.50, `benchmark-simulation-762.md:56`) and marked it her question 2. So on the days a desk cares about, the "win over B2" leg of *Supported* is the easy one and the "win over B1" leg is a calendar-avoidance test; the candidate-dependent size of the B2 leg (24.6% vs. 9.6%, `b2-leg-size-922-result.md`) is the same symptom. **Recommendation (avenue A):** B1′ as the primary benchmark; B2 kept with a declared minimum pool (fall back to all types below ~30 rows, as `POOL_MIN = 30` at `spread_mr_variants.py:45`).

Every post-2020 tail episode is in 2024–26, and each starts on or within a day of a quarter-end, month-end, mid-month settlement or tax date. That is the whole upper-tail sample the confirmation era resembles.

**Is 250 days detecting 3% realistic?** The repo's studies already say no: #733, the design in force has 0.0% power at 3% and at 10% (`confirmation-design-733-result.md`, D0); #754, a 3% gain needs ~62,000–70,000 independent days at 80% power and the history holds 7 effective 250-day windows (`power-replay-754-result.md:25,531-532`); #886, at the post-2020 values power at 10% skill is 9.6% (`postcovid-886-result.md` §2 (d)). My computation adds the mechanism, verified on proxy (d in the candidate orientation):

- ACF of d, post-2020-04: 0.73 (lag 1), 0.48 (2), 0.36 (3), 0.25 (5), 0.28 (10), 0.31 (21), 0.16 (42); 2021–23: 0.89, 0.76, 0.65, …, 0.40 at lag 42. Full sample: 0.19 at lag 1, because the September 2019 days (kurtosis 1283) swamp the covariance — which is why full-history calibration looks benign.
- Long-run-variance ratio post-2020: NW lag 10 gives 4.9; lag 40, 11.9; lag 100, 19.3. The lag-10 standard error (`HAC_LAG = 10`, `scoring_protocol.py:107`; `_long_run_variance`, `:374-387`) is understated ≈2× (2.3× in 2021–23, 1.7× in 2024–26).
- Politis–White block length: 8.6 on the full sample, **108 days post-2020, 114 in 2021–23**. `BLOCK_LENGTH = 10` (`:72`) and `WINDOW_BLOCK = 10` (`:103`, used at `:702-707`) are an order of magnitude short.
- Effective sample at 250 days post-2020 ≈ 250/19 ≈ 13; non-overlapping 250-day windows post-2020: 5.4.
- Null generators, 95th percentile of the NW(10)-studentized 250-day mean (post-2020, 20,000 draws): circular-10, 2.37; stationary mean-10, 2.93; **contiguous windows, 6.05** (125 days: 2.77 / 3.32 / 5.89). Under no true skill 43% of contiguous 250-day windows exceed the circular value and 41% the stationary one. This is what #886 found ("size near 5% only under that generator"), with the cause named: the generator cuts the dependence at the block.

**Why d is this persistent.** B2 is a trailing-year climatology; when the level drifts (yearly means 2022 −11.6 bp, 2023 −9.1, 2024 −6.9, 2025 −2.9; verified) B2 lags for months while B1 tracks, so d is signed for months. The gbm shows the same pathology in 2021–23 (`h5-step3-diagnoses-result.md`). Any candidate with regime-level error has a d with ~100-day memory; no 250-day test can be calibrated from five such windows.

**What a quant would do instead** (avenue B, §4.2): calibrate on contiguous windows and print their count; move the unit of evidence to pre-declared event strata (~100 a year: month-ends, quarter-ends, tax dates, coupon settlements — H6, "not yet queued", `plan.md:74-80`, which should precede H5's registration); a 2-year window with a fixed-b statistic, which needs no block choice (#754's 23% fixed-b size at 250 days is the 250-day problem, not the statistic's); and a scale-free score (avenue C). **Hierarchical pooling across H9 targets does not help:** TGCR − IORB and BGCR − IORB have 0.995 correlation of daily changes with SOFR − IORB and 0.99 of levels (verified) — the same series to within a 0–4 bp wedge; EFFR, ON RRP and SRF take-up are different objects and would need a shared-mechanism model to pool at all.

### 3.4 Structural versus statistical

**What happened in #542/#547/#653/#689** (verified from `structural_liquidity.py:1-56` and the result pages): the structural model is `P(pressure on T) = logistic(b0 + b1·spread + e·(b2·shortfall + b3·tga_up + b4·tax + b5·window))`, `e = exp(−(reserves/assets − 0.12)/0.01)`, six ridge-logistic coefficients — a *classifier* for the retired +5 bp judge. #547 scored it under the judge (fail); #587/#597 found a fitted satiation level is a no-op (a constant factor the logistic absorbs); #626/#653 kinked the curve so the level could bite; #671/#689 found the kink level is not identified by the logistic's objective (`identified-level-result.md:63`). Nothing in that lineage forecasts a distribution of the spread, so "would a small structural model beat the gbm on tails and regime change" has not been asked of H5's target. The nearest thing, the one-parameter MR model, is already within unpaired distance of the gbm (3.2).

**Would it?** Suspected judgement: on tails and regime changes, yes in the sense that matters — a declared-form model degrades gracefully where a 159-input gbm trained on 250–1,900 rows does not (the April 2020 ridge blow-up and the 2021–23 narrowness are both in `upper-bound-result.md`). On pooled CRPS, probably no better than `spread_mr_ctx`. The model I would declare (avenue D): spread_T = L(state_T) + Σ_type β_type·1{T is type}·g(announced net settlement_T) + u_T, with L the regime level (floor-anchored when as-of ON RRP take-up exceeds a declared threshold, else the trailing ordinary median), u an AR(1) with regime-dependent scale, and the tail above the SRF rate a censored exceedance with a leak probability that is a declared function of state (24 days above SRF post-2020: enough to set it, not to validate it). Every input is public by 16:00 ET: the calendar; announced settlement (#541's ruling); ON RRP take-up (Desk result, ~13:15 ET same day; `materiality.md` lists its two holes); H.4.1 reserves per release, Thursday 16:30 ET (information-set rule 1, `:28-31`). The 2021–23 diagnosis found the gbm's loss there is "regime transfer" and that shrinking to persistence closed it; a ~10-parameter model has less to transfer. Kill: no gain over `spread_mr_ctx` on risk-date targets with the interval excluding 0, after one declared fit.

### 3.5 Evaluation hygiene

- **Overlapping leads.** One number per decision day is the mean over h = 1..5 (`day_scores`, `scoring_protocol.py:226-279`), so consecutive d_t share four targets: MA(4) by construction, which block 10 covers. It does not cover the ~100-day regime memory above. **Tracked: #949** (blocks 5/10/20); the reading should be that 20 is still too short and calibration must be contiguous (avenue B).
- **Refit every 21 days** (`information-set.md:64`; `spread_distribution_634.json:56`; `spread_mean_reversion.py:45`) on an expanding window from a 61-day minimum. The 2021–23 problem was not cadence but an expanding window that keeps 2019; the loss closed with a trailing shrink-to-persistence weight (+8.3%, `h5-step3-diagnoses-result.md`), which no cadence would produce. **Tracked: #950 item 6, parked** — I agree with parking it.
- **Variant counting.** 358 candidate files, 86 declared variants plus 84 named arms, 231 files with no committed evidence (`docs/pivot/progress.md:9-13`), against 17 independent +5 bp episodes. The registration publishes the count (§2) but cannot correct for it: development skills are selection-biased by an unknown amount — one more reason the confirmation test needs power and the benchmark must be honest.
- **Bootstrap block vs. actual autocorrelation.** Block 10 vs. Politis–White ~108 post-2020; NW(10) se understated ≈2×; calibration windows built from 10-day blocks. The repo saw the symptom (#830, #873, #886, #913, #922: five recalibrations in six days, 250-day values from 1.35 to 5.71) without naming the generator as the cause.
- **Leakage through feature engineering.** Hygiene is good: imputation means fitted on training origins only (`ml.py:2702-2739`); GARCH fitted on training spreads and only filtered forward (`:2691-2700`); the standardiser fitted on the training design (`_standardizer`, `:4746`); derived rolling features read through a `Causal` view that raises `LookAheadError` on any later index (`derived_features.py:48-75`); the MR level a trailing median at or before the anchor (`spread_mean_reversion.py:74-93`); the blend weight chosen on trailing days whose targets were public (`spread_mr_variants.py:12-14`). Two acknowledged optimisms are labelled: calibration masking for slow declared columns (`information-set.md:71-80`) and current-vintage outcomes (queued). One **suspected** item: B1's and B2's trailing windows are counted in calendar years and clipped at the panel's first day (`protocol_benchmarks.py:138-145`, ruling 6), so the first 2018–19 decision days — the largest scores in the history — are scored against benchmarks built from 3–18 months of data, which flatters candidates there. Development figures only.

## 4. Briefs for the HIGH-value items

### 4.1 Avenue A: the honest benchmark and the missing paired test

**What and why.** Make the zero-parameter calendar-aware persistence (B1′: anchor = last ordinary-day spread at or before the as-of anchor; trailing-two-year changes `s[end] − last_ordinary[end − h]`, pooled by the target's reporting day type with a 30-row minimum, else all types) the benchmark the MME is read against, and run the paired grid-CRPS test of `gbm_funding` against B1′ and against `spread_mr_ctx`. Until it exists, "H5 beats the phase 2 models" is undefined: the transparent models are the phase 2 answer a quant writes first.

**Point-in-time implementation.** Inputs the panel already holds: SOFR (NY Fed, 08:00 ET next business day), IORB (announcement-dated, zero-day lag, `docs/decisions/iorb-availability.md`), the calendar columns, the holiday table. Anchor and guard: `protocol_benchmarks._anchor` / `_require_public` (`:89-135`); `b1_recentred_forecast` (`spread_mr_variants.py:392`) already does the anchor replacement, so B1′ is that plus `spread_mr_ctx`'s context pools. Revision: none beyond the queued first-print rule. As-of read: unchanged from B1.

**First experiment.** H5 step 5 / H1 step 3. On the 1,855 development decision days to 2025-12-31: paired grid CRPS of `gbm_funding` (scratch walks rerun once, declared), `spread_mr_ctx`, B1′, B1, B2; the repo's studentized bootstrap plus the contiguous calibration of 4.2; pooled, by target type, by anchor type, by regime, upper-tail twCRPS. Statistic: skill of `gbm_funding` over B1′ and over `spread_mr_ctx`, ordinary-target cell as headline.

**Kill condition.** If `gbm_funding`'s skill over `spread_mr_ctx` on ordinary targets has a one-sided 95% lower bound below 0, the gbm is not the candidate: register `spread_mr_ctx` (or avenue D's model) and spend the model budget on the strata of 4.2. If its skill over B1′ is below 3% pooled, the MME as set is unreachable by this candidate and the registration should say so.

### 4.2 Avenue B: calibrate on what exists, and change the unit of evidence

**What and why.** The critical values are calibrated on windows that cannot contain the dependence real windows have (3.3). (i) Calibrate `c` on the contiguous windows of the post-2020 development history (every start day; ~5 independent at 250 days) and print the effective count beside the value, so a reader sees that a 250-day `c` rests on five observations. (ii) Register H6 (risk-date severity) ahead of H5's pooled registration: ~100 pre-declared events a year, with per-event d close to independent across events, so a 2-year confirmation gives ~200 events — a sample.

**Point-in-time implementation.** No new data. `window_t_statistics` (`scoring_protocol.py:710-735`) takes `block`; the contiguous variant is `block = days` with starts restricted to `[0, n − days]` (no wrap) on the same d series. The event strata are the calendar columns the as-of rule already admits as scheduled inputs (information-set rule 2, `:32-44`), coupon settlement dated from the announcement (#541; `spread_mr_variants.py:7-10`).

**First experiment.** H1 step 3: recompute `c` at 125/250/500 days on contiguous post-2020 windows for d = S(B1′) − S(cand), cand ∈ {B2, `spread_mr_ctx`, `gbm_funding`}; report size under contiguous, stationary-10 and circular-10 generators side by side (proxy: 6.05 / 2.93 / 2.37 at 250 days). H6 step 1: the H5 metric restricted to declared risk-date targets, paired against B1′ and B2, with the between-event ACF of d reported (expected near 0 at one event's lag — suspected).

**Kill condition.** If contiguous-window `c` at 250 days is below 3.0 on the candidate's own d (the proxy's 6.05 was a B2 artefact), the 250-day design survives and this costs one script. If it is above 4, no 250-day pooled test can confirm anything short of a 20–30% skill, and the registration should be rewritten around the strata and a 500-day window.

### 4.3 Avenue C: a scale-free primary score

**What and why.** Divide each decision day's grid CRPS (candidate and benchmarks alike) by a pre-declared as-of scale: the trailing-year median grid CRPS of B2 on the target's day type, from outcomes public at the decision instant (the last 6 pool positions excluded, as #733's D4 did). Then d_t is O(1) every day; 2019-09-18 (B1 = 297.6 bp) counts as one bad day rather than 6% of eight years; 2021–23 (variance 2.3–5.2 bp²) counts as much as 2025 (59 bp²); skill = mean(d)/mean(b) is a regime-balanced ratio.
