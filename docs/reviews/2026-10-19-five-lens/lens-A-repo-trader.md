## 1. Executive summary — top 3

**1. The month-end and quarter-end "risk window" sits on the wrong days (HIGH, new).** The repo's month-end day type is the month's last *two* business days (`src/repo_model/data.py:2647`, `MONTH_END_BUSINESS_DAYS = 2`; `evaluation_splits.py:131–154`; protocol ruling 6, `docs/decisions/scoring-protocol.md:87`), and its balance-sheet run-up is the last *three* (`balance_sheet_days.py:46`, `RUN_UP_DAYS = 3`). On the verified panel the second-last business day is an ordinary day (non-quarter-end months, 2018–19: mean +0.3 bp, share > 5 bp 7%, against +1.3 bp / 21% on mid-month ordinary days; 2024-09..2026-09: −1.5 bp / 12% against −4.0 / 6%), while the last business day (+11.5 bp / 71%; +2.7 / 12%) and the **first business day of the next month** (+8.1 / 57%; +2.1 / 20%) are the hot days, and the second business day is still hot in 2018–19 (+5.3 / 48%). Quarter-ends behave the same way: last day +2.9 bp mean, share > 5 26%; first day after +0.7 / 24%; second- and third-last −4.6 / 9% and −3.9 / 12% (*computed*). A desk calls this "the turn": the overnight trade that spans the period-end date and unwinds the next morning. Consequences for H5: B2's month-end climatology pools 102 cold days with 69 hot ones; the mean-reversion candidate's "level" (median of the last 10 ordinary days, `spread_mean_reversion.py:36`) excludes the cold penultimate day and includes the hot first days; the gbm's `days_to_month_end` input and H6's risk-date set carry the same mislabel. #677/#731 found the far side (first three market days, +1.0% CRPS at h = 3–5, `docs/research/log.md:505–513`) but nobody has moved the near side. One calendar function, one day; no leakage (a function of the date).

**2. 15 October is a missing risk date (MEDIUM, new).** `tax_date` marks only the corporate estimated-tax months 4, 6, 9, 12 (`data.py:2769`). 15 October is the extended corporate and individual filing deadline, the first mid-month coupon settlement of the fiscal year, and in both scarce-reserve regimes it printed: 2019-10-15 +25 bp (October 5th–13th mean +4.6) and 2025-10-15 +15 bp (mean −1.2) (*computed*; per-year table in §4.1). Both dates appear in the repo's own list of onsets that no risk-date model can warn at h = 2–5 (`docs/pivot/desk-standard-result.md:179`). Cost half a day; the upper-tail twCRPS and H6 are where it pays.

**3. For repo, gross coupon settlement by type beats net-of-maturities, and the size signal is regime-conditional (re-assessment feeding #951; tracked).** On coupon-settlement days inside a regime, the Spearman correlation of the spread with the *gross* coupon offering is +0.50 (2018–19, n = 25) and +0.51 (2022H2–24, n = 34), against +0.32 and +0.27 for the net figure the `net_settlement` module builds (*computed* with the repo's own `net_for_day`, snapshot `20261008T205048Z_568c69347867.json`). Unconditionally across regimes the correlation is ~0 because sizes doubled while pressure fell. A desk finances the gross new issue (dealers and levered buyers take it down; the maturing cash goes to money funds, who decide separately whether to lend it); "net" measures reserve drain, not balance sheet. #951's step 5 should nest gross coupons × the scarcity state, with the 15th / month-end type, not `net_settlement`. Zero cost: it changes the arm before it is declared.

### 3.2 How SOFR is built, and what it implies

**Verified** (NY Fed, *Additional Information about Reference Rates*, https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/reference-rates/additional-information-about-reference-rates): TGCR = overnight specific-counterparty tri-party GC secured by Treasuries, Fed counterparties excluded; BGCR = TGCR + GCF; SOFR = BGCR + FICC-cleared DVP, after removing "20 percent of the lowest-rate transaction volume from the DVP segment", pro-rated at the 20th percentile rate, and affiliated trades. The rate is the rate at the 50th percentile of cumulative dollar volume, rounded to 1 bp; the 1st, 25th, 75th and 99th volume-weighted percentiles and the volume (nearest $1 bn) are published alongside, but not on contingency days. Revision at about 14:30 ET only if the change exceeds 1 bp, same day only. **Verified** (Operating Policy 2024-11-25, https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/opolicy/operating_policy_241125; request for comment, https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/reference-rates/request-for-public-comment-on-proposed-modifications-to-sofr-methodology): before 2024-11-25 the trim was "rates below the 25th volume-weighted percentile rate", which removed 20–25% of DVP volume on most days and as little as 7% on some; the fixed 20% rule and the affiliate exclusion apply from the 2024-11-25 publication (22 November data) and earlier prints are not revised; the NY Fed reports the old and new rules print the same about 93% of days since 2020. The repo carries no such event (`metadata/policy_events.json` has none; *verified* by grep).

**Which segment moves SOFR on a stress day.** The practitioner's instinct is "DVP". The panel says the GC leg: sd(TGCR − IORB) = 9.7 bp against sd(SOFR − IORB) = 10.0 and sd(SOFR − TGCR) = 1.5; on the 71 days with spread ≥ 10 bp the DVP premium averages 2.9 bp against 1.75 overall, and 63% of those days have TGCR − IORB ≥ 10 too; 2019-09-17 printed SOFR = TGCR = 5.25% (*computed*). The premium is a period-end phenomenon (quarter-ends mean 2.6, up to 8 bp on 2024-09-30). So: the level is tri-party; what DVP contributes is the *dispersion* (p75 and p99) and the volume (DVP is most of the 2.8 tn), which is where sponsored and basis-trade financing shows up. TGCR differs from SOFR by that premium and nothing else; BGCR ≈ TGCR at 1 bp rounding on nearly every day because GCF is small.

**Is the repo's use consistent?** Mostly yes: `sofr_iqr_bps` = p75 − p25 (`data.py:86`), p99 − IORB and p99 − p75 as tail measures (`early_warning.py:98–153`), and `gbm_funding` reads p25, p75 and volume (`backtest_gbm_conformal_pid_nested_funding.json:1049`). Three caveats. (i) p1 is not a floor measure: after the DVP trim it is the lowest surviving rate, near TGCR or the ON RRP rate. (ii) The percentiles' meaning shifted on 2024-11-25 (A11). (iii) `sofr_volume` enters as a level (A8); its tripling is structural growth, and a tree fitted on 2018–23 levels sees 2025 volumes as out of range. The 15:00 next-day availability declared for SOFR, TGCR, BGCR (`sources.json:487–531`) is correct and conservative relative to the 08:00 print and 14:30 revision window. First-print scoring is already queued (excluded here).

### 3.4 The backstops

**Verified** (SRF FAQ 2025-12-12, https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/repo-agreement-ops-faq-251212; FAQ 2025-06-25, https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/repo-agreement-ops-faq-250625): two daily operations, 08:15–08:30 and 13:30–13:45 ET, funds "within thirty minutes following the operation close", full allotment, $40 bn per proposition per security type, rate set by the FOMC at the top of the range (3.75% on 2025-12-12 against IORB 3.65%: a 10 bp ceiling over IORB); results published "after the completion" with no clock time; counterparties are primary dealers and SRP depository institutions. The repo's register has the facility's creation, its rate steps and the December 2025 removal of the aggregate cap (`policy_events.json`, kinds `standing_repo`), but not the 26 June 2025 morning operation (`nyfed_srf` limitation text mentions it; the register does not).

**Shape of the right tail.** Before 2021-07-29 there is no ceiling: 2018-12-31 +60 bp, 2019-09-17 +315. From 2021-07-29 the ceiling is IORB + 10 bp plus a stigma premium that was large while the facility settled only in the afternoon (a dealer that bids at 13:45 has already funded its book), smaller after the morning operation, and smaller again once the cap went. The ceiling leaks on snapshot dates because an SRF repo expands the bank's balance sheet on the day it is trying to shrink it: 2025-12-31 printed +22 bp with the facility in place. A model should therefore treat the tail as a mixture: bounded on non-turn days from the facility's operative date, unbounded on turn days; and the facility-term dates (2021-07-29, 2024-12-30 extra operations, 2025-06-26, 2025-12 full allotment) belong in the register as announced events, read from their announcement. The upper-tail diagnosis (#756) found q95 exceedance 0.095–0.128 against 0.05, i.e. too thin: the bound helps by re-allocating width from ordinary post-2021 days to turn days, not by narrowing further. A5 is medium-low because the 0.975 and 0.99 grid levels carry little CRPS weight.

### 4.1 A1 — relabel the turn

**What and why.** Replace "month-end = last two business days" with "turn = last business day of the month and first business day of the next month" (quarter-end: the quarter's last day and the first day after; year-end: 31 Dec, the first business day of January, and 26 Dec as its own flag). |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018–19 mean bp / share > 5 | −0.1 / 7% | +0.3 / 7% | **+11.5 / 71%** | **+8.1 / 57%** | +5.3 / 48% | +1.3 / 21% |
| 2024-09..2026-09 | −1.6 / 12% | −1.5 / 12% | **+2.7 / 12%** | **+2.1 / 20%** | −0.8 / 16% | −4.0 / 6% |
| 2020–21, 2022–24 (abundant) | flat | flat | flat | flat | flat | flat |

Quarter-end months, all years: 3rd-last −3.9 / 12%, 2nd-last −4.6 / 9%, last +2.9 / 26%, first after +0.7 / 24%.

**Point-in-time implementation.** A function of the date and `metadata/market_holidays.json`, like `data.quarter_end` (`data.py:2606–2642`); `last_business_days_of_month(…, count=1)` already exists (`data.py:2650`). **First experiment.** H5 step 2, development tier. (a) Re-run B2 with the turn label and report how its CRPS moves (B2 is a benchmark: the protocol's change control, §13, applies if it is to be adopted — the evidence is a kind-(a) demonstration that the current label misranks). ### 4.2 A2 — 15 October (and the 15th family)

### 4.3 A3 — gross coupons × scarcity for #951's arm (re-assessment)

**What and why.** #951 step 5 re-runs `gbm_funding` with "the calendar and net-settlement inputs". The panel says net adds nothing over gross on repo days and the size signal is regime-conditional (§1 item 3, §3.1). Within-regime Spearman on coupon days: gross +0.50/+0.51 vs net +0.32/+0.27 (2018–19, 2022H2–24); on bill-only days gross +0.5–0.6 in 2020–24 is an issuance-trend confound and net is ~0.
