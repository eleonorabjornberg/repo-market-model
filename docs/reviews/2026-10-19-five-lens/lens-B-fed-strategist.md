## 3. Lens-specific findings

### 3.1 The demand curve, the "ample" boundary, satiation distance, the scarcity state, the H7 estimator

**What the repo encodes.** The scarcity state is an ordinal 0–3: the reserves ratio (H.4.1 WRESBAL over H.8 total assets of all commercial banks) scored against a fixed band `SATIATION_BAND = (0.12, 0.13)`: 0 at or above 13%, 1 inside, 2 below 12% (**verified**, `src/repo_model/scarcity.py:109`, `:173-183`); plus the ON RRP balance against a $100bn buffer (**verified**, `:122`, `:186-191`). Satiation distance (`satiation_dist_roll`) and the structural model's elasticity `e = exp(-(ratio - 0.12)/0.01)` hang off the same 12% (**verified**, `src/repo_model/structural_liquidity.py:12-17`, `:97-98`; `src/repo_model/satiation.py:89-91`). The liquidity repo's H7 estimator is `D_T = R_T − k_T` with `k_T` the least-squares kink `spread = a + b·max(0, k − ratio)` on all training pairs to the refit cutoff (**verified**, `repo-market-liquidity/src/repo_liquidity/h7_estimator.py:3-6`, `:134-136`, `:145-176`).

**Is the boundary read correctly?** The headline numbers are read correctly from the source. Afonso, Giannone, La Spada & Williams (SR 1019, revised November 2025) state satiation "when reserves exceed 12-13 percent of banks' assets" and that the slope "becomes increasingly negative as reserves decline from ample to scarce" (**verified**, https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr1019.html). The ON RRP buffer rule is also the right mechanism: Anbil, Anderson, Cohen & Ruprecht ("Stop Believing in Reserves", 2023) find that in the excess-liquidity regime the repo rate sits at the ON RRP rate while reserves exceed a threshold (about $2.9tn at IORB 4.65%), and that non-bank liquidity demand, not bank reserve demand, binds (**verified**, https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2024/program/paper/7QSdHhaG). That is the 2021–23 regime exactly, and it is why the state reads "abundant" on every decision day of that window (**verified**, `docs/pivot/h5-step3-diagnoses-result.md:17`).

**Where it is too crude, and what a practitioner would do.**

1. *The object is a slope, not a band.* AGLW's finding is a time-varying elasticity with vertical and horizontal shifts ("reserve demand has shifted over time, both vertically and horizontally", **verified**, SR 1019 abstract). The repo collapses it to a fixed band plus a kink fitted in levels. Stage 2 of the liquidity repo found the curve shifted up between 2018–20 and 2025 (**verified**, `repo-market-liquidity/PLAN.md:213-219`), and 2025's pressure came at ratios 0.117–0.129, inside and above the band (**verified**, `src/repo_model/satiation.py:5-6`). A single intercept `a` across the history absorbs the vertical shift into the kink. Fix: estimate on daily *changes* with a slowly varying slope (the RDE's construction), or at least one intercept per corridor-width regime.
2. *The dependent variable.* AGLW, the RDE and Lopez-Salido & Vissing-Jorgensen use the fed funds rate minus IORB (**verified**, LSVJ: "the main estimated spread is EFFR − IOR", https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/conferences/shared/pdf/20231004_mon_pol_conference/Lopez_Salido_paper.pdf; RDE: "how much the federal funds rate responds to shifts in reserve supply", https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2024/20241017). SOFR − IORB carries collateral and settlement effects that are not reserve demand. `fit_level` minimises squared error over every pair (**verified**, `satiation.py:180-193`), so 2019-09-17 at +315 bp and the quarter-ends dominate. Fix: fit on calendar-clean days (the panel already flags month-end, quarter-end, tax and coupon-settlement days), or on EFFR − IORB (`effr_minus_iorb_bp` exists, **verified**, `src/repo_model/contract.py:410`), or with a least-absolute-deviation hinge.
3. *The supply measure.* LSVJ instrument with ln(reserves + ON RRP) and scale demand by deposits (**verified**, same PDF). The repo's ratio excludes ON RRP and uses H.8 assets as the scale; ON RRP enters only as a $100bn binary. A desk's supply variable in 2021–24 was reserves + ON RRP, because MMF cash at the Fed is one phone call away from private repo.
4. *The H7 estimator cannot beat R except where the kink moves.* Its step-1 record says so (**verified**, `repo-market-liquidity/docs/stages/h7-step1.md` §2); Stage 6 found candidates A and B ranking-equivalent to R (**verified**, `docs/research/hypotheses/H7.md:30`). With fixes 1–2 the kink moves for the right reasons rather than from a quarter-end.

**Does the repo use the NY Fed's published elasticity, and should it?** It does not; the literature page lists it with "Check whether it can be downloaded" (**verified**, `docs/pivot/literature.md:12`), and #950 parks it (**already tracked**). Publication facts, verified today: monthly, "around 10:00 A.M. Eastern Time on the third Thursday of each month", moved to the first business day after an FOMC blackout if it falls inside one; the first release (2024-10-17) carried data "as of Friday, October 11, 2024" (**verified**, https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2024/20241017). Revision behaviour: "We estimate the RDE daily using only information available as of each day, making our estimates equivalent to real-time calculations" (**verified**, https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/?p=32246). So the back-history is filtered, not smoothed, and a later release does not revise an earlier day's estimate; what it cannot do is make 2010–2024 values public before 2024-10-17. Read rule: training history only before 2024-10-17 (the rule the repo applies to the back-filled SOFR history, **verified**, `docs/decisions/information-set.md:93-103`); from then on, at 16:00 on day d read the latest release dated ≤ d, use its estimates to its data-as-of date, carry forward; staleness 6 to about 40 days. Should H5 use it? No: a monthly, lagged, regime-level series cannot move a 1–5-day CRPS; #950's "low" is right. Should H7m? Yes, as the external benchmark for goalposts 1 and 3: the Fed's own real-time ampleness signal is the object H7m estimates. That is a stronger use than #950 describes. Whether a download with vintages exists I did not verify (**suspected**: the research page shows a chart only).

### 3.4 TGA forecasting the strategist's way

What the repo reads: the weekly H.4.1 TGA (`tga`, Wednesday level, read the Friday after the Thursday 16:30 print, **verified**, `docs/decisions/information-set.md:28-36`) and, in measurement runs, the daily DTS balance (#763, no gain; already raised). `projected_reserves` rolls reserves forward with announced settlements and the tax-date inflow and otherwise assumes persistence: "No announced TGA path is tracked, so the persistence rule applies" (**verified**, `src/repo_model/projected_reserves.py:22-24`).

How a strategist builds the path: start at today's DTS closing balance; end at the refunding assumption for quarter-end ($950bn end-September, $850bn end-December 2026); between them lay the known calendar: corporate tax dates (the 15th of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, April 15 individual), coupon settlements on the 15th and month-end, bill settlements Tuesday/Thursday, and the debt-ceiling path when the limit binds (drawdown to a few tens of billions, then the rebuild after resolution, June 2023 and July 2025, when the daily drain ran tens of billions for weeks; **suspected** magnitudes, from memory). The refunding target fixes the *drift*; the calendar fixes the *timing*.

What it adds beyond the realized TGA at h = 1–5: the sign and size of next week's drift when the gap to target is large (post-debt-ceiling rebuilds; the December drawdown), and nothing on an ordinary week, where the daily flows are already in the settlement and tax-date inputs. That is why #950 rates it low–medium, and I agree. Two additions to the parked description: read the gap as a daily drift, (target − current DTS)/business days to quarter-end, informative only when |drift| exceeds about $10bn/day; and add Treasury's debt-ceiling (X-date) letters to Congress as the dated source of the drawdown–rebuild path, which the refunding assumption misses because a refunding statement in a debt-ceiling quarter assumes resolution. The register holds only the statutes (**verified**, nine `debt_ceiling` entries, all acts).

### 3.5 The 2021–23 loss: the right model for an abundant-reserves regime

**First, the loss itself has moved.** Under protocol v1 `gbm_funding` is level with B1 in 2021–23 (−0.007 [−0.129, +0.109]; **verified**, `docs/pivot/h5-step4-result.md:39`); the resolved loss is variant (c)'s (−30.4% [−54.7%, −13.5%]) and the mean-reversion candidate's (−2.6% [−7.3%, +1.5%]) (**verified**, `docs/pivot/h5-step3-diagnoses-result.md:9`; `docs/pivot/evidence/spread-mean-reversion/report.md:49`). "Every gbm loses" was a phase-2 statement.

**What the regime is** (**verified**, panel, `work-B/panel_checks.py`): 748 days, mean −9.81 bp, standard deviation 2.39, variance 5.73 bp² (against 458 in 2019), lag-1 autocorrelation 0.935; 70.2% of days have a zero daily change and 93.4% a change within ±1 bp; 46% of days print exactly −10 bp (the IORB − ON RRP width); one day above 0 bp in 2021 and none in 2022–23; SOFR at or below the ON RRP offering rate on 66.6% of days (96.8% in 2022, when it traded through the floor by up to 11 bp at month-ends because the per-counterparty cap and ineligible lenders left cash outside the facility).

**Is "no forecastable signal; forecast the floor" the honest answer?** Half of it. There is no *reserve-driven* signal: the state is "abundant" on every decision day and reserves fell from $4.2tn to $2.8tn across the window (**verified**, panel) without moving the spread. But "forecast the floor" literally is wrong: a constant law at ON RRP − IORB has MAE 1.28 bp at h = 1 against 0.40 for persistence (**verified**, panel). The honest model is **persistence with a floor-aware, discrete, calendar-dipping law**: anchor at yesterday's print, residuals from floor-regime days only, no mass above 0 bp, and the month-end dip below the ON RRP rate as the one calendar effect. In a floor regime, forecast the gap to the floor, not the gap to IORB: #948's transform plus item 1's residual pool.

**Regime-switching, two-part, or abstain?**
- *Hidden-state switching* (#384 tried an HMM under the judge; nothing merged) adds nothing, because the state is observable: the as-of scarcity state is 0 throughout and the floor flag is on from 2021-04-21 to 2023-12-11 (**verified**). Estimating a latent state the Fed publishes wastes degrees of freedom.
- *Two-part by an observable, announcement-dated switch* is the right form, and the machinery exists (the `two_part` structure of #632/#634). Serve the floor-regime law when the flag is on, the candidate otherwise. In 2021–23 it scores at worst B1; on the upper tail it removes the −39.6% that mean reversion suffers from putting mass above 0 bp against B1's 0.009 (**verified**, `evidence/spread-mean-reversion/report.md:59`).
- *Abstaining* is a trap under the protocol: a missing forecast is scored as B1 "and it is counted. More than 5% missing voids the registration" (**verified**, `docs/decisions/scoring-protocol.md:257`). A registration that abstained through a floor regime would be voided. Implement abstention as an explicit B1-equivalent forecast, never as a gap.

**Would it score better?** On development days only marginally pooled: it removes a drag of about half a percent, since `gbm_funding` is already level there. In the 250-day confirmation window it matters only if a floor regime returns, which needs ON RRP take-up back in the hundreds of billions, i.e. balance-sheet expansion well beyond RMPs: low probability within 250 days. Its value is a guard and an honest "performance by regime" paragraph in the validation report (H5 step 6), not a score driver.

### 4.1 Floor-regime flag: regime-pooled residuals and a two-part rule (medium)

**What and why.** An as-of indicator of "SOFR pinned to the ON RRP floor" built from the two series that define it, with (a) B1-style and MR-style residual pools drawn only from same-regime days and (b) a two-part rule that serves the persistence law when the flag is on. Why: the regime is observable and announced; residual pools that mix regimes are too wide in the floor regime (B1's 50% interval covered 58.9% in 2021–23, **verified**, `h5-step3-diagnoses-result.md:13`) and too narrow after it (the 2024–26 gain below).

Revision: SOFR's 14:30 same-day revision is inside the 15:00 declaration; the register is never revised. ### 4.2 H7 kink estimator re-specified (medium for H7m; low–medium for H5)

**What and why.** Keep `D_T = R_T − k_T`, change how `k_T` is estimated: (1) fit on calendar-clean pairs (exclude quarter-end windows, month-end ±2, tax dates and coupon-settlement days, all flagged in the panel) or on EFFR − IORB; (2) a least-absolute-deviation hinge instead of least squares; (3) one intercept per IORB − ON RRP width regime (25/20/15/10/15 bp, announcement-dated). Why: the kink and slope of a least-squares hinge on raw SOFR − IORB are set by a few hundred-bp days (**verified**, `satiation.py:180-193`), which is the mechanism behind the 4-point refit jumps of liquidity finding #54 and the "not identified" ties of #747.

**Point-in-time implementation.** No new source: the panel's calendar columns, `effr` (`nyfed_effr`, 15:00 next day, **verified** `metadata/sources.json`) and the register.
