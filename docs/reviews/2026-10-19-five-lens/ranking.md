## Where the lenses converge

Four findings arrived from more than one reviewer, independently, which is the strongest evidence this review produces.

- **Skill against B1 is mostly calendar avoidance.** The trader (A) shows the turn is mislabelled; the quant (C) shows a persistence that merely declines to copy calendar spikes beats B1 by 15.6%; the validator (D) reads the headline the same way from the repo's own splits (+20.7% falls to +9.0% on ordinary anchors, and the near-blind pass rests on two days). Fixing the calendar and fixing the benchmark are the same correction from two sides; do them together.
- **The upper tail is where the model is both mis-calibrated and over-weighted.** D: 90% coverage 0.695 and clustered misses; C: the 23-level grid gives the 1%/99% levels 2.5× the integral's weight on an integer target; A: the SRF bounds the tail on non-turn days from 2021-07-29 but leaks on snapshot dates (2025-12-31 printed +22 bp). A calibration gate, a scale-free score and a turn-aware tail are three parts of one fix.
- **The candidate reads raw levels that cannot transfer across regimes.** D (bill level sign flips, reserves at $1.5tn vs $3tn, volume tripled), A (`sofr_volume` as a level, p1 is not a floor measure), E (reserves and TGA are week averages, not the Wednesday levels the docs describe). The relative encodings are the same publication instants, so this is one declared variant.
- **The regime switch is observable and announced, not latent.** B (floor-regime flag from SOFR and the ON RRP rate), C (a corridor model with a regime-anchored level; HMM and hierarchical pooling waste degrees of freedom). Both say: two-part by an announcement-dated switch, never abstention.

One disagreement in emphasis, not in substance: the validator would register B3 (calibrated, passes Kupiec pooled at h=1) now; the quant would first test whether `gbm_funding` beats `spread_mr_ctx` at all. Both say the uncalibrated candidate as it stands should not be registered.
