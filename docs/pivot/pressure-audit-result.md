# The pressure-day audit: what the work may be overlooking (#473)

A scratch measurement under the pressure-day judge (`metadata/pressure_judge.json`), unchanged. It writes nothing into
`docs/runs/` and moves no published figure: scored days are 2018-06-29 to 2025-12-31 (the judge's shared grid, on which all
five horizons are scored, runs from 2018-07-06 and holds 26 onsets), no 2026 day is read (`docs/decisions/lockbox.md`), and
nothing joins a candidate list or a published declaration. The rule it scores is declared in `metadata/pressure_audit.json`,
committed before any score; one amendment, the hindsight ceiling of section 3, was committed after a dry run on the benchmark
rows had shown the walk-forward rule's first result and before the scoring run this page reports (the rule, its clauses and its
choice did not change). The tables are generated by `scripts/pressure_audit.py` into
[`evidence/pressure-audit/`](evidence/pressure-audit/) and copied here.

Every hit so far comes from 2019 and 2025. The four onsets of 2018, the two of 2020 and the two of 2024 are warned by no
candidate. This page asks why.

## Result in one paragraph

The bar's minimum lead is a **two-trading-day-ahead forecast from the last spread anyone can see**, issued one business day
before the pressure day, not a three-day one (section 1). The five tier-1 passers keep their recall at that horizon and lose
it from lead 2 on (13 to 14 of 26 at lead 1, 8 to 10 at lead 2 or more). The misses of the three years have three different
causes, and only one of them is a missing input. In 2018 every model and both benchmarks are **blind**: the flag cut-off is
chosen on a training window that holds no onset it can catch (the first onset of the sample is the day the sample starts), so
it flags nothing, whatever the inputs say. In 2020 the two March onsets are on ordinary days with no settlement, and the
passers are constructed to forecast exactly zero on ordinary days. In 2024 the first onset, the quarter-end of 2024-09-30,
is warned by a calendar rule chosen on training windows alone and by none of the model-based rows. A calendar and settlement
rule is no substitute for them: it warns 8 of 26 at 3.04 false alarms per onset and, chosen in hindsight under the false-alarm
limit, 5 to 7 of 26.

## 1. The effective horizon

**What the bar asks.** A flag has *lead h* when it is made at 16:00 on the h-th business day before the scored day
(`pressure_judge.py`, module docstring; `asof.InformationRule.decision_instant`, `asof.py:497`). Tier 1 asks for 50% of the onsets
flagged at some horizon h >= 1 (`metadata/pressure_judge.json`, `tiers.onset_warning.lead_at_least`), so the nearest warning is
the one made at 16:00 on the business day before the pressure day.

**What that decision can see.** `InformationRule._latest` (`asof.py:534`) reads, for each field, the latest row whose declared
first-observable instant is at or before the decision. SOFR for a day is declared public at 15:00 on the next business day
(`metadata/sources.json`, `nyfed_sofr.release_lag`, lines 519 to 526; the New York Fed publishes it at about 08:00 and the
declaration waits for its revision window), so at 16:00 on `T-1` the latest SOFR is that of `T-2`; the spread of `T-1` prints on
the morning of `T`. `baseline._check_decision_relative_availability` (`baseline.py:3029`) checks the same thing independently on
every fold, and the training labels end the same way (`pressure_judge.choose_cutoffs`, `pressure_judge.py:938`: the outcome of the
decision day is read from the business day before it). Measured on the scored days (Table F, row "spread"): the rule reads the
spread **2 panel days before the scored day on all 1873 scored days at h = 1**, and h + 1 days before at horizon h.

**So the minimum lead is a two-day-ahead forecast, not a three-day one.** From the last observed spread (day `T-2`) to the
pressure day `T` is two trading days, and the day in between (`T-1`) is not observed. A forecast at lead h is an (h + 1)-day-ahead
forecast from the last spread; **the three-day-ahead call is lead 2.** `#445` found the same gap from the other side (a perfect
nowcast of the unpublished day would move the median's best lag from 2 to 1; `nowcast-result.md`).

## 3. An upper bound from calendar and settlement information alone

**The rule** (`metadata/pressure_audit.json`, declared before any score). Warn on a day when any enabled clause holds: it is a
quarter-end; it is a tax date; it is within N calendar days of month end (N one of 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7); its gross Treasury settlement
is at least S billion (S one of 75, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300), a clause available at h = 1 only, because a settlement is public at
15:00 on the panel day before it. At each 21-day refit and horizon the clauses are chosen from the refit's training window alone, as
the judge chooses a flag cut-off (`pressure_audit.select_clauses`): the combination with the highest onset recall whose false
alarms per onset on the window are at most 2, of equal recall the one that flags fewer days. The window is the judge's: the
scored days whose outcome was public at the refit's first decision instant. Two variants: `calendar` (no settlement clause) and
`calendar_settlement`. The *ceiling* rows choose the same combinations once on all scored days, in hindsight; they are not a result
and carry no claim about the future.

## 4. Why the three years are missed

* **2018 (4 onsets): the cut-off warm-up, not an input.** The judge chooses the flag cut-off on the forecast's own earlier
  probabilities, from the first scored day, and flags nothing when the window holds no onset it can catch within two false alarms
  per onset (`metadata/pressure_judge.json`, `cutoff_rule.no_onsets`, `none_meets_limit`: `never_flag`). The panel starts on
  2018-04-03, when SOFR does, and the first onset of the sample is 2018-06-29, the first scored day. The four onsets of
  November and December 2018 therefore meet a window with no usable onset, and the four passers and both benchmarks are blind on
  all four, and the fifth passer, `risk_quantile_skewt_base`, on one (Table 5). The reason is the window, not the inputs: the
  cut-off has no onset to be set on. A hindsight rule over the calendar columns warns 2018-11-30.
* **2020 (2 onsets): ordinary days, outside the passers' construction.** 2020-03-04 has no settlement at all and 2020-03-12 has a
  78 billion bill settlement; neither is a quarter-end, tax date or month end. The five passers forecast exactly zero on days that
  are not risk dates (`risk-date-severity-result.md`), so they cannot flag them, and the benchmarks, which can, do not. No
  calendar, settlement, reserve, TGA or ON RRP value in Table 2 ranks both onsets well and consistently; the SOFR 99th percentile
  above IORB does (0.96), and the T-bill rates and the SOFR percentile levels rank them at 0.82 to 0.90, which in March 2020 is
  largely the level of rates falling.
* **2024 (2 onsets): one is a calendar day the models miss.** The quarter-end of 2024-09-30 is warned at every horizon by the
  calendar rule chosen on training windows alone, and by none of the model-based rows, none of which was blind on it. At h = 1
  their probabilities on the day were 0.00 (`risk_gbm`, `risk_gbm_base`), 0.01 (`risk_logistic_base`) and 0.13
  (`risk_quantile_skewt_base`), against cut-offs of 0.12 to 0.27 (`calendar_climatology`: 0.20 against 0.29), after a quiet
  2021-23. That is a regime and calibration effect on a calendar day, not a missing input. The other, 2024-12-26, is reached by no row, calendar rule or
  hindsight ceiling included.

## 5. Recommendation

Ranked by the evidence above. It recommends; it adds no input, changes no declaration and settles nothing for Eleonora.

1. **Make the model reach ordinary days with an input already in the repository: the SOFR 99th and 75th percentile above IORB**
   (`sofr_p99_iorb_bps`, `sofr_p75_iorb_bps`, #377). They are public by 4 pm exactly as the spread is, they rank the onsets of all
   three missed years better than the spread level does (0.72, 0.96, 0.62 against 0.71, 0.79, 0.41), and they are the policy-adjusted
   input that ranks both 2020 onsets highest (the level inputs that rank them as high mainly follow the falling rate level). The risk-date models already read them but use them on risk dates only, and their
   twins without them did as well (`risk-date-severity-result.md`), so the test is not "add the input" but "read it on every day".
   This is the one that could reach 2020 (2 of 26) and, with a bigger probability on a quarter-end, 2024.
2. **Fix the cut-off warm-up before reading anything into 2018.** Four of the 26 onsets (15%) cannot be flagged by any model
   under the declared cut-off rule because the rule has nothing to learn from. This is a property of the bar, not of an input: the
   history before the first onset does not exist on this panel. The options are to report tier 1 from the first year in which a
   window can hold an onset, to seed the window from the longer pre-SOFR history (#129), or to leave the 2018 onsets as the
   unreachable part of the sample. Choosing among them changes how the bar is read, which is Eleonora's.
3. **Date the settlement size from its announcement.** Treasury announces the offering 2 or more panel days ahead for 99 to 100%
   of offering dollars; the registry dates it from the auction results, so the clause is unreadable at h >= 2
   (`treasury_auction_net_settlement` already dates it from the announcement as a measurement field, and #426 found no gain from
   its net figure). The gain is bounded: at h = 1, where the size is fully known, the settlement clause adds no onset in the
   walk-forward run and two in hindsight (2019-03-29, 2025-12-26), neither in a missed year. This would be last of the three.
4. **Not first: a better same-day estimate of the unpublished day.** #445 found a perfect nowcast, an inadmissible ceiling, moves
   the best lag from two days to one and lowers the CRPS by a tenth, and the nowcast it declared did not help. The effective
   horizon is a fact of publication (section 1) and no input made public by 4 pm removes it.
5. **No missing level input stands out.** Reserve balances rank the onsets of all three missed years well (inverse AUROC 0.89,
   0.87, 0.66), the daily TGA and ON RRP rank them in the same direction, and every candidate already reads reserves, the scarcity
   state and the weekly TGA. They carry the slow state, not the day.
