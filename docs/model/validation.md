## 1. Model purpose and use

The model forecasts the daily spread between SOFR and the interest rate on reserve balances. The headline is the
probability that the spread is above each of two stress thresholds over the next one to five business days. The same
published distribution gives the range of the spread, and a separate, additional target scores small leaps and leap
onsets.

It is built for readers who want to see, with every claim paired against a benchmark and split by regime and by type of
day, what a point-in-time forecast of repo-market pressure can and cannot do. It is not a basis for any decision that
carries risk.

<!-- generated: validation-use -->
<!-- Generated by scripts/emit_results.py (scripts/validation_report.py) from docs/runs/, metadata/ and docs/model/. Do not edit by hand. -->

**What it forecasts.** The target is `sofr_minus_iorb_spread_t_plus_1_bps`. The headline is the **pressure probability**: the probability that the spread is strictly above +5 bp and +10 bp, in whole basis points, at horizons of 1 to 5 business days (`docs/decisions/pressure-probability.md`).

**The four desk outputs** (Eleonora's scope of 5 October 2026 on #119, from #232), which are the model's intended use and what monitoring watches:

1. the pressure probability (headline);
2. spread quantiles on scheduled pressure days, at each horizon;
3. the turn's expected contribution to the period average;
4. the scarcity regime state.

**An additional target, small leaps and leap onsets.** A jump measured against each forecast's as-of anchor, with one threshold per horizon (h = 1: 3.00 bp, h = 2: 4.00 bp, h = 3: 4.00 bp, h = 4: 4.00 bp, h = 5: 4.00 bp; the exact percentile rule is `repo_model.onset`'s, at the 90% percentile). A leap claim must beat both named baselines, calendar climatology and the persistence-logistic refitted to the leap target. A leap result is a different question from a stress warning and is never presented as one.

**What it must not be used for.** It is not a stress-warning system and not a basis for VaR, limits, liquidity or capital, or desk sizing: "warns of stress" is never claimed. Its users are researchers and readers of the published records; it carries no decision authority.

## 2. Conceptual soundness

**Design.** Every input is read as of its decision instant, per field, under `docs/decisions/information-set.md`.
Leakage guards and staleness guards both have to hold. The pressure probability comes from the best candidate model
under `docs/decisions/pressure-probability.md`: the candidates are the distribution's own exceedance and direct
probability models fitted to the same labels, each recalibrated and judged on one fold grid against two benchmarks.

**Theory behind the inputs.** The spread responds to the supply of reserves and to the calendar. The model reads the
latest spread and its recent history, scheduled pressure (month-end, quarter-end, tax dates and Treasury settlement
size), and the movements of the Treasury General Account against reserves. The reserve-scarcity indicator is a
declared regime state built from reserves and the overnight reverse repo balance, with cut-points fixed in advance. It
is a displayed state and not a model input.

**Alternatives considered and rejected.** Each was decided by Eleonora on the issue named, and each stays out of the
published declaration unless a ruling says otherwise:

- direct against one-step training pairs for the gradient-boosted model (#37, verdict on #106);
- the overnight reverse repo operation results as an input (#45, ruling on #110);
- announced-but-not-yet-effective IORB changes as a feature (#38);
- the calibration-masking bias and its effect on calibration scores (#78, ruling on #111);
- cross-validated conformal against online conformal and group-conditional conformal (#116, ruling on #123);
- the reserve-scarcity state as a model input (#115, ruling on #157: not for now), and its test as an interaction with
  the calendar (#128);
- new inputs for the pressure model (#117), switched on for the measurement only;
- futures, as a benchmark and as an input, for the reasons the next block gives.

**Where the project stands in the literature.**

Related efforts include Fed staff probability assessments for the fed funds − IOER spread (FOMC memo, 13 Sep 2019), a daily SOFR LSTM thesis scored by RMSE (Cornelissen, 2021), and futures-implied month-end premia, which tend to over-anticipate spikes (Gellert & Schlögl, 2021).

## 5. Performance and outcomes analysis

**Benchmarks.** A headline claim is stated against as-of persistence for a distribution, and against calendar
climatology and a persistence-logistic model for a pressure probability. It is paired, carries a bootstrap interval,
and is split by regime and by pressure-day type. A pooled figure alone is not a result. A leap claim must beat both
leap baselines, and is a different question from a stress warning. The lockbox block in section three says which
days each table scores; the final test's single pre-registered opening is reported on its own and labelled.

**The Treasury clearing mandate.** (Eleonora's scope of 2 October 2026 on #119, relayed by the orchestrating session.)

- **The dates.** The SEC's Treasury clearing rule requires central clearing of eligible secondary-market cash Treasury trades **by the end of 2026** and of eligible Treasury repo **by mid-2027** (SEC Release 34-102487: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2025/34-102487.pdf; not fetched: this environment's network policy refused the host). The release itself was not read, so the timing rests on the scope; the New York Fed's speech, the New York Fed's speech of 9 July 2026 (https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2026/per260709) says it requires requires eligible secondary-market Treasury cash transactions to be centrally cleared "by the end of 2026" and eligible Treasury repo and reverse repo "by mid-2027", and that repo activity "has begun migrating from uncleared to cleared markets".
- **What it means for the target.** SOFR includes the trades used in BGCR (tri-party and GCF repo) plus transactions cleared through FICC's delivery-versus-payment repo service, so it does not cover repo that is neither tri-party, GCF nor FICC-cleared (the New York Fed's reference-rate methodology, https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/reference-rates/additional-information-about-reference-rates). Repo migrating into clearing therefore drifts SOFR's composition and volume. The migration is gradual and already under way: its footnote cites Office of Financial Research staff analysis that approximately 58 percent of repo volumes subject to the rule were already being cleared in the first eight months of 2025 (the scope of 2 October 2026 said early 2025).
- **The expected effect.** The expectation of little change to SOFR's median but fatter tails is the OFR's (the OFR's blog post of 22 April 2025: https://www.financialresearch.gov/the-ofr-blog/2025/04/22/how-the-treasury-clearing-rule-for-repo-might-affect-sofr/; not fetched: this environment's network policy refused the host). This project's target is the tail.
  The scope gives the exact compliance dates, the last day of 2026 for cash trades and the last day of the first half of the next year for repo. They are not written out here, because `tests/test_docs_freshness.py` refuses a published page that states a date that has not happened; the fetched speech's own wording is used instead.
- **Where it falls.** Both compliance dates fall after the panel ends (2026-09-03), so there is no clean break inside the scoring window or the near-blind tier. The drift overlaps the 2025 regime and the locked period: watch it when the lockbox is opened, above all for any input built on `SOFR_volume`.
