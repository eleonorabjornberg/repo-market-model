## Rulings on the implementation (13 October 2026, PR #660)

Eleonora ruled on the questions the implementing pull request asked. The ruling was relayed by the orchestrating
session on PR #660 (comment 6145669436); it is recorded here as relayed, and this record is in force only once she
merges it.
<https://github.com/eleonorabjornberg/repo-market-model/pull/660#issuecomment-6145669436>

1. **The interval is the studentized stationary bootstrap.** H1's coverage check found the plain percentile
   bootstrap of §5 covering far below its nominal rate on a resampled real series of daily differences. §5's
   interval is therefore read as the studentized stationary bootstrap: each resample's mean(d) is studentized by its
   own Newey-West standard error at lag 10, with mean block 10 and 10,000 draws, and the one-sided bound is the
   observed mean less a quantile of those t-draws times the observed HAC standard error. The coverage check is re-run
   on the same real series and on a control, and both are reported in the pull request. No candidate is registered
   until #656's power study confirms coverage under the heavy-tailed series.
2. **B3 is a secondary comparison on its own levels.** The incumbent forecasts five quantile levels (0.05, 0.25,
   0.5, 0.75 and 0.95). It is scored on those five against each candidate read at the same five. B1 and B2 stay the
   primary benchmarks, on the 23-level grid.
3. **Scoring confirmation days opens them.** Every confirmation day of §1 falls in the blind tier of
   `docs/decisions/lockbox.md`. Scoring a registered candidate's prospective days at a checkpoint is the opening of
   those blind-tier days. Each opening is Eleonora's own attested action (#256); neither this record nor the relay
   opens any day.
4. **Interim checkpoints use 100,000 draws.** The checkpoints stay at 60, 125 and 250 days with Lan-DeMets
   O'Brien-Fleming spending. The first checkpoint's nominal one-sided level is about 6 in 100,000, below what 10,000
   draws resolve, so the bootstrap at the 60- and 125-day checkpoints uses 100,000 draws. (The relay chose more
   draws over dropping the first checkpoint, which leaves the decided spending plan unchanged; she may override.)
5. **The critical value is calibrated by resampling** (a second ruling of 13 October 2026, relayed on PR #695,
   comment 6146940245, after the studentized bound still covered about 0.83 on the real series). The studentized
   bound's critical value is calibrated by the double (iterated) stationary bootstrap on the same mean block: each
   outer resample is itself resampled, and the outer t-draws are read at the level that makes the inner bound cover
   its nominal rate in the bootstrap world. Nothing in d is trimmed or winsorized. The outer level keeps 100,000
   draws at the interim checkpoints. Registration stays held until H1's coverage check passes and #656's power study
   confirms it. <https://github.com/eleonorabjornberg/repo-market-model/pull/695#issuecomment-6146940245>
6. **The other readings stand as the pull request proposed them**, unless she says otherwise:
   - the futility bound's level is not set by §6 and is given explicitly at each verdict;
   - the verdict reads the primary score alone at the spent level, and Holm across the primary and the key secondary
     is reported, not applied to the primary;
   - the key secondary's weight is the threshold weight 1{z > 0 bp}, by the chaining function max(z, 0);
   - B1 adds the h-day change to the last public value, as §4 reads, although that value is one row older than the
     decision day;
   - a decision day counts as missing if any lead is missing, and a target with no published outcome drops that lead
     alone, the same lead for every forecast;
   - B2's day type is the reporting day type (month-end on the market calendar), with no minimum sample;
   - the trailing windows are counted in calendar years back from the decision day and clipped at the panel's first
     day.

**Draft scoring protocol, v1 (orchestrating session, 12 Oct 2026, for Eleonora's decision). Designed from first principles; current rules are kept only where they survive scrutiny.**

## 0. What it decides
Each hypothesis (H1–H10) gets exactly one of four verdicts: **supported**, **killed**, **inconclusive (continuing)**, or **not testable as stated**. Nothing else counts as a result. A pass on days we have already seen is never a verdict.

## 1. Which data can be evidence
| Tier | Days | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Development | 2018-06-29 → 2026-09-03 | Unlimited exploration and model choice. **Never evidence for a verdict.** It covers 2026-01-01 → 09-03, which was opened on 2026-10-05 (#592). |
| Unused | 2026-09-04 → the registration date | Not scored for any verdict. The outcomes are public, so we cannot show that forecasts made now for those days are untouched. |
| **Confirmation** | Business days after a candidate's registration | **The only evidence.** Each forecast is produced by the live pipeline at the decision instant. It is committed, with hash and timestamp, before the outcome is published, and scored afterwards. |

*Replaces:* verdicts from judge passes on 2018–25 (about 200 variants on 26 onsets, #567), and the 2026 Jan–Sep "confirmation window" (#585 and #593), whose days have been opened.

## 2. Registration (per hypothesis, before its first confirmation day)
- **Committed before the first confirmation day:**
  - one **primary candidate**: code commit, configuration, inputs and training rule, frozen by hash;
  - at most two **secondary candidates**;
  - the primary metric, benchmarks, minimum meaningful effect (MME) and checkpoints;
  - the development variant count to date.
- Registration is **Eleonora's attested action**. Nothing about a registered candidate changes after that. A changed candidate is a new registration with a new start date.

## 3. Primary goal, H5: the SOFR − IORB distribution in bp
- **Target.** SOFR − IORB as first published, in bp. Leads h = 1–5 business days. Decision instant as in the information-set rule (kept: leakage control is not a scoring choice).
- **Forecast.** Quantiles at a fixed grid of 23 levels: 0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.10, …, 0.90, 0.95, 0.975 and 0.99.
- **Primary score.**
  - CRPS, computed as twice the mean pinball loss over the grid;
  - averaged with equal weight over h = 1–5 for each decision day;
  - giving one number per day.

  *Replaces:* the 5-level CRPS, which is too coarse to see the tails, and the h = 1-only primary cell.
- **Key secondary.**
  - Threshold-weighted CRPS on the upper tail, with weight 1{y > 0 bp}. That is spread above IORB: enough days for power and the region a desk cares about.
  - It is tested at the same α, with Holm across the primary and this one.
- **Reported, not decision rules:**
  - Brier score for P(> +5) and P(> +10) read off the distribution;
  - coverage at 50, 80, 90 and 98%;
  - PIT histograms.

## 4. Benchmarks (one tested module; logged live alongside the candidates)
- **B1, as-of persistence.** The last public value, plus the empirical distribution of that horizon's own h-day changes over the trailing two years.

  *Replaces:* the one-step residual law, which does not widen with horizon. That flatters long leads.
- **B2, calendar climatology.** The empirical distribution of the spread over the trailing year on days of the same type (quarter-end, month-end, tax date, ordinary).
- **B3, incumbent.** The best registered model at the time. Initially, the published v1 funding declaration.
- **Rule.** "Supported" needs a win over both B1 and B2. "Improves on what we have" also needs a win over B3.

## 5. The test
- **Daily difference.** d_t = S(benchmark) − S(candidate). Positive always favours the candidate, and this is the one sign convention everywhere.

  *Replaces:* three conflicting conventions (#604, #613).
- **Skill.** mean(d) / mean(S(benchmark)).
- **Interval.**
  - stationary bootstrap, mean block length 10 business days (it covers the 5-day forecast overlap and spread persistence);
  - 10,000 draws, with the seed fixed at registration;
  - **one-sided 95%**.
- **Cross-check.** Diebold–Mariano with HAC (Newey–West, lag 10), reported.

*Replaces:* 2,000 draws and two-sided 90%, with block lengths of 2, 10 or h+1 depending on the code path.

## 6. Verdict rule, effect size and timing
- **Checkpoints.** 60, 125 and 250 confirmation days. The overall one-sided α of 0.05 is spent across the three checkpoints with Lan–DeMets O'Brien–Fleming spending.
- **Supported.** At a checkpoint, the lower bound of mean(d) is above 0 against B1 and against B2, **and** the point skill is at least the MME.
- **Killed (futility).** At a checkpoint, the upper bound of skill is below the MME against B1.
- **Otherwise inconclusive.** Continue to the next checkpoint. If still inconclusive at 250 days, the verdict is "not shown".
- **MME, proposed: 3% CRPS skill against persistence.** A power study on development data, run before the first registration, checks that 3% can be detected within 250 days. If it cannot, the study reports what can, and Eleonora sets the MME once before registration.

*Replaces:* the absence of any effect-size rule, and "interval excludes zero" with no notion of a meaningful effect.

## 7. Splits
- Results are reported by the **as-of scarcity state** (public at the time) and by day type.
- No split is a pass requirement: there is too little power. A verdict states the conditions it was confirmed under.

*Replaces:* calendar-year regimes drawn in hindsight as decision splits. Their declaration is "provisional" and was drawn after the fact.

## 8. Multiplicity
- Within a hypothesis: no correction for the primary candidate, Holm for the secondaries and the key secondary metric.
- Each hypothesis carries its own α. The plan reports how many hypotheses are under test, and the development variant count is published with every registration.
- H9 applies Holm across its targets.

## 9. The other hypotheses
| H | How it is scored |
|---|---|
| H1 judge / scorer validity | Pre-registered synthetic tests: perfect, persistence, shifted, under- and over-dispersed, and lead-misaligned forecasts. The scorer must recover the known order. **Pass or fail, before any registration.** |
| H2 signal in held data | Development only. A go/no-go for effort (the upper-bound study), **never a claim**. |
| H3, H4 new data, longer history | Nested prospective test: the H5 primary candidate with and without the new inputs. Same metric and rule. |
| H6 risk-date severity | The H5 metric restricted to declared risk dates, prospectively, against B1 and B2. Same rule. |
| H7 scarcity tracking | Prospective: does the scarcity estimate improve H5's primary score as an input (nested test)? |
| H8 ranking | Prospective: the precision of the top 5 of the next 20 business days, against the calendar of known risk dates. Same test machinery. |
| H9 richer targets | The same machinery per target, with B1 and B2 on that series (ON RRP in $bn), Holm across targets. "Leads pressure" is a nested test: the target's forecast as an input to H5. |
| H10 attribution | Descriptive. No verdict. |

## 10. The +5 bp judge
**Retired as a decision rule for hypotheses.** Its five tiers mix detection, calibration, false-alarm budgets and cut-off tuning, with known defects (#615, #616, #617). They have been redefined three times in four days. The +5 and +10 bp probabilities are read off the distribution and reported (section 3). What happens to the registered classifier pre-registration (#593) is Eleonora's decision.

## 11. Missing data
- A candidate must log every business day.
- A missing forecast is scored as B1's forecast, so it cannot gain, and it is counted. More than 5% missing voids the registration.
- A day with no published outcome is dropped for every forecast.
- The outcome is the first published value.

*Replaces:* no rule.

## 12. Must exist before the first registration
1. One scoring module implementing sections 3–6 and 11, tested on synthetic forecasts (H1).
2. Prospective logging for registered candidates and for B1–B3, with commit timestamps, extending the existing live record.
3. The power study (section 6).
4. An independent check by the forensic auditor that 1–3 match this text.

## 13. Change control
- This becomes `docs/decisions/scoring-protocol.md`, version 1, merged by Eleonora (attested).
- It changes only on one of two kinds of evidence:
  - **(a)** a synthetic or analytic demonstration that it misranks forecasts of known quality;
  - **(b)** a reproduced error in its data or code.
- Each change also needs the auditor's confirmation and Eleonora's attested action.
- **No change may be motivated by a candidate's result.**
- A change applies only to confirmation days logged after it. Verdicts already reached stand under the version they were reached with.
