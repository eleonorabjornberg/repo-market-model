## The answer

1. **Where.** The design rejects 11 of 200 tests; 10 are independence tests (the other is the Kupiec q99 test of 2021-23), and 9 of the 11 are at lead 1 (the other two are at leads 2 and 3 in 2021-23). The dependence is a one-day-apart effect: lift at lag 1 (observed hit pairs over those expected under each regime's own rate; 1 is independence) is 5.6 [3.8, 7.6] at q05 and 7.0 [4.7, 9.5] at q95, lead 1, and falls to about 2 at lags 2 to 4 (table below).
2. **The design's spacing leaves one innovation shared.** A forecast for D + h reads the spread to the panel day D - 1 (information-set rule 1: the daily rates are read at the row two panel days before the target). Two forecasts the design keeps h days apart therefore share the innovation of the first one's target day, so a forecaster that is calibrated by construction shows dependent hits at that spacing. Read at spacing h + 1 instead of h, the same cells give 0 independence rejections (against 10), and 1 test in all (the Kupiec q99 test of 2021-23); at h + 2, 0 and 1. In the simulation, a calibrated forecaster that reads to D - 1 has a mean lag-1 lift of 2.6 (AR(1) with phi 0.3, which matches the candidate's lag-1 error correlation of 0.29) to 4.8 (phi 0.9) at lead 1 and is rejected by the design in 83.0% to 100.0% of paths, against 8.3% at spacing h + 1; the control that also reads D is rejected in 6.3%. The previous day's hit and residual are not public at the decision, so no model can feed them back: this part is not removable by a model change.
3. **What is left after the structural part.** The candidate's lag-1 lift (5.6 and 7.0) is above the calibrated reference at the matched phi (2.6), and the lift stays near 2 at lags 2 to 4 where the reference has none. That excess is a miss rate that moves in time: the standardised absolute error autocorrelates at the public lags (0.44 [0.25, 0.57] at lag 2, 0.52 [0.30, 0.62] in 2021-23), the signed error does not carry one level the model misses (lag-2 correlation -0.19 [-0.28, -0.03], negative pooled; positive in 2020 and 2025-26, negative in 2018-19, 2021-23 and 2024), and the last public spread's gap to the median has a pooled slope of -0.36 [-0.64, -0.10], negative, which is the opposite of a level that adapts too slowly (positive only in 2020: 0.31 [0.13, 0.54]).
4. **Calendar and pressure days.** Hits on ordinary target days run well under the regime-expected rate (q05: 14 against 33.0 expected) and on calendar or pressure days above it, and 73% of lead-1 target days are not ordinary, so almost every hit pair involves one. The rate difference between adjacent days does not explain the dependence: with the rate fitted by regime, target-day type and pressure type, the lag-1 lift is still 4.5 [3.1, 6.2] at q05 and 4.6 [3.1, 6.1] at q95. Pairs of two ordinary days are too few to read (expected pairs under 1).
5. **Model changes.** None of the five post-hoc adjustments improves pooled grid CRPS: every point estimate is a loss (the least bad, A2 rescale (60), changes it by 0.0118 [-0.0010, 0.0254]; positive is worse) and the interval excludes zero on the loss side for A3 both, A4 recentre, negative slope allowed. None removes the independence rejections (A1 recentre: 11 of 200, A2 rescale (60): 17 of 200, A3 both: 17 of 200, A4 recentre, negative slope allowed: 12 of 200, A5 rescale (20): 10 of 200; the candidate: 11). The scale adjustments gain in 2021-23 (the interval excludes zero) and lose in 2018-19 and, for A2, 2025-26. The ranking and the question for her are below.

## 5. Model changes, ranked by evidence

Nothing is fitted or registered here; the change is hers to pick (the question is in the pull request). The effects below are the adjustments' paired figures above, and are estimates of expected effect, not results of a model.

0. **Not a model change: read independence at spacing h + 1.** The thinned rows of the design at spacing h share the innovation of one target day under the two-row information lag. Effect expected: independence rejections 10 to 0, all tests 11 to 1; grid CRPS unchanged. This amends amendment 4 and is hers; it is listed first because it is the only item the evidence supports as removing the rejections.
1. **A scale that reacts faster to recent errors (A5, then A2).** Evidence: |z| autocorrelates at the public lags, and the adjustments that scale are the ones with a gain in 2021-23 (A5 -0.017 [-0.026, -0.007], A2 -0.011 [-0.021, -0.001]). Expected effect on grid CRPS h = 1-5: 0.0142 [-0.0009, 0.0298] for A5 and 0.0118 [-0.0010, 0.0254] for A2 pooled (no pooled gain; losses in 2018-19 and 2025-26; after the first 250 days A5 changes it by 0.0051 [-0.0105, 0.0211]); on the coverage cells: 10 and 17 rejected against 11, the lead-1 independence rejections stay. It addresses the persistent part (lags 2 to 5), not lag 1.
2. **A calendar- or pressure-day scale.** Evidence: hits on ordinary days are well under their expected rate and on calendar or pressure days above it; but the typed lift is 4.5 and 4.6 at lag 1, so day-type rates do not explain the dependence. Already measured by #1402 (a residual scale by pressure-day type: undecided or a loss on the development window), not repeated here; no new figure. A pattern this study adds: the tails move in opposite directions on some days (hits per expected hit at q05 and q95 on dom_19_23 days: 1.42 at q0.05 and 0.12 at q0.95; on quarter_end days: 2.62 at q0.05 and 5.08 at q0.95), which is a level or skew misplaced on those days and not a scale alone.
3. **A level term on the latest public error (A1, A4).** Evidence against: the sign changes by regime, and pooled the lag-2 correlation of z is negative (-0.19 [-0.28, -0.03]) and the gap slope is negative (-0.36 [-0.64, -0.10]). A1 (slope clamped at zero) does nothing at lead 1; A4 (negative slope allowed) changes grid CRPS by 0.0275 [0.0081, 0.0507] (worse) and leaves 12 rejections. Not recommended.
4. **Feeding the previous day's hit or residual (lag 1).** Not available: the outcome is not public at the decision instant. A change cannot use it without changing the information set (her decision, `docs/decisions/information-set.md`).

## What was not checked

- A fitted model: nothing is fitted, registered or proposed as a parameter set. The adjustments are online rules on the candidate's output with settings fixed in the declaration; a different window, clamp or slope rule could move their figures.
- The 2026 days (near-blind tier), B1, B1', B2, B4 and the uncapped secondary version; development data only, and a development figure is not a verdict.
- A dependence-aware independence test or a different thinning for leads 2 to 5 in 2021-23 beyond spacing h + 1 and h + 2; the power of the design at spacing h + 1 for a dependence like the candidate's is not measured (its thinned series are shorter).
- The simulation is an AR(1) with one regime, normal innovations and no volatility clustering or calendar effects; it shows what the information lag does to a calibrated forecaster's hits, not how much of the candidate's lag-1 lift it explains. The matched-phi reference is a reading, not a decomposition.
- The typed rate is fitted in sample to the hits it is then compared with (about 16 parameters on 100 to 130 hits), so the typed lift is biased downward a little; its interval holds the fitted rates fixed.
- Whether the information-set rule itself (rows read two panel days before the target) is the intended reading at the 18:00 instant for the SOFR-based target; the study takes the registry's release lags as they are.
- Intervals for the day-type hit ratios other than Wilson on the rate; days are serially dependent, so those intervals are too narrow.
