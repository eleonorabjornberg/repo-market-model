## 1. H5: a predictive distribution that stays calibrated across a break (step 3, 2021–23 and the upper tail)

What the recalibration track already did ([`recalibration-track-result.md`](../pivot/recalibration-track-result.md)): per-threshold
isotonic and Platt recalibration, Platt per scarcity-state by day-type group, a regime-weighted Platt after Barber et al., conformal PID
and nested selection. All of it adjusts a distribution after the model has produced it, one threshold at a time. The
items below differ in one of two ways: they put the state inside the model or the conformal update, or they change what is combined
(weights that vary by quantile or by time). The tail items change what is measured, which is cheap to adopt first.

### What bears on the three families of #754

- **(a) The stationary-bootstrap test.** None of the items tests the stationary bootstrap directly. Bauer finds skewed small-sample statistics make both normal and bootstrapped critical values unreliable near 250 observations on asymmetric losses. Frederiksen et al. show normal-limit procedures break with infinite-variance differentials, and a bootstrap rests on the same finite-variance logic: estimate the tail of the CRPS differences before trusting it.
- **(b) A self-normalised or HAC Diebold–Mariano test.** Coroneo and Iacone support fixed-b critical values at T of 50 to 100 and warn that a persistent differential can remove power from any mean-based test. Lerch et al. give the variance choices for k-step horizons. Power at 60 to 125 days depends mainly on the dependence and tail weight of the differential, which no choice of test changes; simulated power should be reported at the dependence and tail estimates of the real series.
- **(c) An anytime-valid sequential test.** Choe and Ramdas with the betting confidence sequence fit best, because they test the average null a mean CRPS comparison asks. A declared cap on the difference is needed unless the asymptotic form is used. Horizons 2 to 5 are open in the theory (Choe and Ramdas) and cost power in Henzi and Ziegel's merged e-values. All sources agree that anytime-valid tests are less powerful than a fixed-sample test at a pre-set sample size.
- **A sign or rank test.** Only Podkopaev and Ramdas use sign payoffs, as a classifier-betting construction. No fetched source quantifies a sign test's size or power under serial dependence or heavy tails: a gap. A sign test targets the median of the score difference, not the mean, so its claim differs from (a) to (c).

## Leads

A lead is a suggestion, not a directive. Queueing any of them is the orchestrator's job, in plan order.

1. **Quantile-wise combination of persistence, climatology and the model, with online weights** (Berrisch & Ziel; Taylor & Meng as a one-parameter variant). Feeds H5 step 3, the 2021–23 remedy ("a shrink-to-persistence weight"): let persistence win near the floor and the model win in the tail. Risk: the weights lag a break, so it may only reproduce what a single shrink weight does; and it adds variants to the count.
2. **A tail-calibration check and a twCRPS above 0 bp computed from samples** (Allen et al., both papers). Feeds H5 step 3's upper-tail sub-question; a scoring and diagnostic change, no new model. Risk: few exceedances, so the interval may be too wide to say anything; the weight function is a choice for Eleonora.
3. **Estimate the tail index and the dependence of the real CRPS-difference series before sizing a confirmation**, and add a fixed-b and a self-normalised subsampling check beside the bootstrap (Frederiksen et al.; Coroneo and Iacone). Feeds #754, families (a) and (b). Risk: with a few hundred days the tail-index estimate is itself noisy, and the answer may be that no test has useful power at 60 days.
4. **An asymptotic confidence sequence on the mean CRPS difference with a declared cap** (Choe and Ramdas; Waudby-Smith and Ramdas). Feeds #754, family (c). Risk: horizons 2 to 5 are open in the theory, the cap is a judgement call, and the power cost against a fixed-sample test is expected to be largest at 60 days.
5. **Conformal prediction with a state predictor** (Sun and Yu), the as-of scarcity state as the state input. Feeds H5 step 3, beyond the recalibration track. Risk: the state is observed with a lag, so the predictor may be weakest exactly at breaks; it is built for intervals and would need one learner per level.
