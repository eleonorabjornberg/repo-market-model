# Executive summary

**One page, for a reader who manages financial risk and does not intend to read
any code.** Everything below is either plainly labelled as an opinion or is
generated from the repository's own run records; the numbered figures in the
"What has been established" block are not typed by anyone.

## The problem

Banks, funds and corporate treasuries finance themselves overnight in the
repurchase-agreement market — lending cash against Treasury collateral. Almost
always it is dull and the cost sits a hair away from the Federal Reserve's
administered rate. Occasionally it is not: in September 2019 and again in March
2020, overnight repo funding jumped away from the policy rate within a day, and
institutions that had assumed cheap rollover funding discovered otherwise.

The question a risk manager actually has is not "what will the repo rate be
tomorrow". It is **"how likely is it that funding costs jump tomorrow, and how
wrong could that estimate be"**. That is a question about a probability
distribution and about the honesty of the estimate, not about a point forecast.

## The approach

This repository builds the machinery to answer that question and to be caught if
it answers it dishonestly. Three commitments do most of the work.

**Only what was knowable.** Every observation carries the moment it first became
public, and every forecast is built from observations that had already been
published at the declared decision time. The most common way a forecasting study
fails is not a broken model; it is a convincing backtest that quietly used a
number nobody had yet. Here that is enforced in code and raises an error rather
than producing a slightly better result.

**Evaluation that follows time.** The model is tested by walking forward through
history, re-fitting as it goes, and discarding the days immediately around each
test day so that a slow-moving series cannot leak the answer into its own
training data.

**Probabilities scored as probabilities.** The output is a distribution and the
probability of crossing pre-declared stress thresholds, scored with the measures
used for probabilistic forecasts rather than with accuracy on a point estimate.

Every input is an official public source, stored as an immutable checksummed
copy, and every published run record names the exact code commit and data file it
came from.

## What has been established

- The forecasting machinery has been run end to end on real market data covering 2018-04-03 to 2026-09-03, and scored at **2080 separate decision points** — each one made using only information a person would actually have held that afternoon.
- On that history, a simple benchmark — tomorrow looks like today — is wrong by **3.14 basis points on average**, and the range that error could plausibly take is 2.60 to 3.79 basis points.
- Its uncertainty bands are **too narrow**: a band meant to contain the outcome 90% of the time contained it 81.0% of the time. That gap is measured and unexplained, and it is the next thing being worked on.
- The scoring itself has been checked against a case where the right answer is known in advance: a forecast with no information in it scores **0.000 skill**, exactly as it must. Every later claim of skill rests on that.

**No model has yet been shown to forecast better than the benchmark.** That is the honest state of the work, and it is why nothing here is a basis for a decision about money.

## The limitations, stated plainly

- **There is no forecasting result yet.** What exists is a verified measuring
  instrument and a characterised benchmark. A model that beats the benchmark is
  the current milestone, not a finished one.
- **The uncertainty bands are known to be too narrow**, and whether that is bad
  luck or a real miscalibration has not yet been established either way.
- **The money-market-fund data channel has open quality decisions**, recorded in
  `DATA_QUALITY_DECISIONS.md` rather than resolved. One of them — a group of fund
  filings that report cash twice — was found by checking an accounting identity
  the pipeline enforces, which is the system working, and it is still open.
- **A full rebuild from a clean clone is not yet possible.** Source snapshots and
  the frozen panel are not redistributed, so an outside reader can reproduce the
  code paths but not yet every published figure.
- Nothing here is investment advice, a trading system, or a risk-management
  control. It is an academic and portfolio project.
